The seven biggest problems

1. No decisive impact frame. Flame Cleave and Earth Break have effects that develop over time, but neither shows a sufficiently explosive, visually distinct moment of contact.

2. Your VFX are mostly outlines. Thin orange rings, arcs, and faint circles describe an attack's area but have almost no visual mass. RO3 uses broad luminous shapes, bright cores, strong contrast, and layered energy.

3. Not enough contrast within individual effects. Mid-orange dominates. You need a dark outer silhouette, saturated red body, brilliant orange-yellow highlight, and a tiny near-white impact core.

4. Weak anticipation-to-release contrast. The screen looks similar before and after an attack. A powerful ability needs a noticeable energy buildup, a violently compressed peak, and a readable aftermath.

5. The Warrior's power identity is unclear. Power Slash, Whirlwind, Flame Cleave, and Blade Cyclone currently share too much of the same orange circular visual language. Their silhouettes should be recognizable even without particle colors.

6. Ground interaction is underdeveloped. Earth Break should fracture the ground. Inferno Crash should leave a scorched crater. Charging Strike should kick up a directional dust wake. Right now too much energy seems detached from the environment.

7. Damage-number overlap masks the FX. The current videos show numbers obscuring the central attack area. Increasing particle count without adjusting number stacking will make this worse.

The biggest problem is not insufficient particles. It's insufficient shape, value hierarchy, impact timing, and environmental reaction.

The main difference from RO3

Your RO3 reference shows attacks with large, clean radial or sword-like silhouettes. The golden circles around Raise Shield and King's Grace have controlled composition. Rolling Cutter produces bright, substantial rotating blades. Its effects are visually strong without covering the whole character.

Your Earth Break has the beginnings of this approach, but the golden sword is currently a thin symbol rather than an enormous, physically threatening weapon.

4. Make the VFX work on snow, grass, and purple caves

The same palette cannot be composited identically on every map.

Snow

Problem: White cores vanish into the bright snow, and gold shockwaves lose their edge.

Correction: Increase dark-rim opacity by +15 percentage points, decrease bloom by 25%, strengthen red body opacity by +10 points. Keep warm cores, but add a narrow dark contour. Snow decals should be brown-gray scorch marks or exposed dirt, not barely visible yellow.

Grass

Problem: Strong reds work, but green vegetation and busy ground textures interfere with thin particles.

Correction: Use the base palette unchanged. Preserve high-frequency detail only in the leading blade or impact core. Ground scorch opacity 30–40%, with soft-edged warm brown marks. Avoid scattering tiny red specks across flowers.

Purple cave

Problem: Deep-red bodies merge into purple terrain, while emissive cores become disproportionately bright.

Correction: Increase orange-yellow highlight area by approximately 15–20%, reduce peak bloom 15%, and shift the outer silhouette toward warm brown-black (#482829). Brighten the body by 8–12% if needed. Keep gold highlights readable against purple.

Implement these as three biome-specific VFX material presets sharing the same underlying meshes and particle systems. They are visual balancing parameters, not three completely separate effects.

This is a conceptual visual order, not one fixed Unity sorting order. Split tall effects into back and front renderers around the character. World-space height, camera depth, and sorting groups must still be respected.

Unity implementation rules

Shader body: Prefer standard premultiplied alpha for saturated red shapes. Pure additive blending washes out against snow and destroys dark silhouettes.

Highlight and core: Additive or premultiplied emissive materials, with controlled HDR emission.

Ribbons: Use custom mesh strips with width taper, asymmetric alpha falloff, and UV scrolling only where needed. Avoid making every ribbon a uniform-width tube.

Shockwave: A world-space annular quad or mesh, using radial alpha masks and soft edge distortion. Prevent texture tiling from making the ring look like a repeated pattern.

Impact flash: A concentrated burst only. Aim for 30–75 ms of peak brightness rather than an extended white screen.

Ground decal: Project or place onto the ground plane and depth-test where appropriate. Avoid decals floating through rock ledges or waterfalls.

Bloom: Set exposure and threshold for all three representative biomes. Avoid judging effects only on black backgrounds.

FX scale: Tie gameplay range to world units and tune visual projection at the standard camera, rather than resizing hitboxes to match screen-space circles.

6. The three changes that will produce the biggest visual upgrade

1. Replace thin lines with thick, sculpted sword shapes.

For Flame Cleave, grow the primary trail from a thin arc to approximately 0.30H. For Earth Break, turn the sword into a broad filled mesh with a solid body and sharp brilliant edge. This alone will materially improve the perceived weight.

2. Create one unmistakable impact peak per skill.

Make the brightest frame short and explosive. Combine sword contact, radial emission, camera shake, and brief hit-stop at the same instant. The current effects have movement but lack an obvious climax.

3. Give every skill a unique silhouette.

Power Slash = sharp crescents. Whirlwind = horizontal double-blade spin. Flame Cleave = forward fire wall. Charging Strike = narrow penetrating spearhead. Earth Break = falling greatsword. Blade Cyclone = tall helical tornado. Inferno Crash = explosive crater. Berserker = vertical transformation aura.
